The lifestyle suggestion algorithm is developed as a rule-based, safety-first layer separate from the insulin-resistance prediction model. The model estimates cardiometabolic risk, while the suggestion engine uses recent check-in data to identify small, actionable behavior opportunities across sleep, movement breaks, diet, alcohol, maintenance, and data support. It first checks whether there is enough recent data. if not, it recommends one easy logging action instead of inferring behavior from missing information.

The recommendations are grounded in public-health and clinical guidance, including CDC sleep guidance, the CDC National Diabetes Prevention Program, the Diabetes Prevention Program clinical trial, ADA Standards of Care, and CDC alcohol guidance. The engine uses conservative triggers, such as short recent sleep averages, repeated low movement-break frequency, repeated food-journal patterns, or alcohol frequency above a defined threshold.

Diet suggestions require repeated patterns, movement advice accounts for physical limitations, and maintenance messages reinforce what is working without implying zero cardiometabolic risk.
